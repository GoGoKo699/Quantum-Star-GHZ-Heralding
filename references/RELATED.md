# Related work

## Three links on the honeycomb lattice

Khanna, Halder and Sen's single-layer construction uses one independent partially entangled qubit pair per edge. A fixed GHZ-basis measurement on one color class produces generalized three-qubit GHZ states. Section III.A, Eqs. (8)–(13) and Figs. 3–6 give the measurement outcomes, their probabilities, the subsequent conversion to perfect GHZ states and the mapping to triangular-lattice site percolation. The exact conversion probability with this readout and optimal local filtering is $q_0=2q^2(q+3(1-q))=6q^2-4q^3$, where $q$ is the smaller Schmidt probability of a link. The corresponding link threshold is $q>0.3263518223\ldots$. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.109.012419) · [Open full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09040)

The [hub instrument](../science/IMPLEMENTATION.md) improves this published primitive on the same input pairs. For the three-link star, it achieves the [optimal hub-only probability](../science/OPTIMALITY.md) $`\min\{2q,6q^2-2q^3\}`$ with unitary leaf corrections. Its strict advantage is over the full probability of the complementary measurement and local filtering, including all complementary outcomes. Using the same independent-star mapping and ideal fusion as in Sec. III.A gives the [achieved link threshold](../science/NETWORK.md) $q>0.3045468490\ldots$. This comparison is for the monolayer construction with generalized hub measurements. The independent-star transition does not bound the performance of arbitrary LOCC over the whole lattice.

Triangular site percolation at occupation $1/2$ and the honeycomb bond threshold $1-2\sin(\pi/18)$ are classical inputs. Acín, Cirac and Lewenstein introduced independent conversion to Bell pairs as a classical entanglement-percolation benchmark and studied changes of network geometry before conversion. Perseguers et al. developed multipartite GHZ conversion and fusion for network percolation. [Sykes and Essam](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1704215) · [Acín, Cirac and Lewenstein](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys549) · [Perseguers et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.81.032327)

For a fixed complementary pair with computational probabilities $p_x$ and $p_{\bar x}$, the Bell cut across any leaf bounds the unconditional contribution from that pair by $`2\min\{p_x,p_{\bar x}\}`$. An amplitude filter attains that bound. The strict odd-star improvement therefore uses a genuinely different measurement component and cannot be obtained by reoptimizing the amplitude balancing within the inherited complementary decomposition.

## Steering and the weakest-link bound

The Hughston–Jozsa–Wootters theorem explains why ensemble decompositions of a fixed leaf density matrix are accessible by measuring its hub purification. Here the new ingredient is the [explicit characterization](../science/OPTIMALITY.md) of the feasible success diagonals for mixtures of local-unitary GHZ states, and a direct measurement that realizes the optimal one. Smolin, Verstraete and Winter analyze asymptotic entanglement of assistance and GHZ distillation, including one-way broadcasting. Their rates concern a many-copy task. [Hughston, Jozsa and Wootters](https://doi.org/10.1016/0375-9601(93)90880-9) · [Smolin, Verstraete and Winter](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.72.052317)

Vidal's single-copy pure-state conversion theorem gives the bound $2q_i$ across the cut between leaf $i$ and everyone else. This cut starts with Schmidt probabilities $(1-q_i,q_i)$ and ends with two equal Schmidt probabilities on success. Hence the smallest link gives the bound $2\min_i q_i$ for arbitrary network LOCC on a star. Where the explicit hub-only protocol attains it, that protocol also attains the unrestricted LOCC optimum. [Vidal](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.83.1046)
